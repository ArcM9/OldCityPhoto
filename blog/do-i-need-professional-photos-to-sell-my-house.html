Do I Need Professional Photos
to Sell My House?

By Ryan McGill  ·  June 2026  ·  5 min read  ·  Old City Photo Co., Knoxville TN

I hear some version of this question a lot. Sometimes it comes from sellers, sometimes from agents relaying what their clients said: "Couldn't I just buy a camera and tripod?" or "My phone takes great photos, how different could it really be?"

It's a fair question. Phone cameras have gotten genuinely impressive. And a cheap camera with a tripod is better than a phone held at arm's length. But that question leaves something out: the camera is the easy part. Learning the lighting, the angles, and the editing and doing all of it well enough to make a home look its best takes years to get good at and years more to master. The equipment is a tool. Knowing how to use it is the skill.

What Phone Photos Almost Always Get Wrong

It's almost always the same two things: lighting and angles. Phone photos are almost always crooked and dark, with a warm, brownish tint because the white balance gets thrown off by the mix of natural light, incandescent bulbs, and overhead fixtures that most homes have. The camera is trying to do everything at once and it compromises on everything as a result.

A professional photographer controls each of those variables deliberately. White balance is set for the room. Exposures are bracketed and blended. Flash is bounced to fill in the shadows without creating harsh highlights. The angle is chosen not just to look good in the frame but to accurately represent the space and its relationship to the rest of the home. None of that happens automatically.

In darker rooms such as closets, bathrooms, and interior hallways, the difference is even more pronounced. These spaces photograph as dim and uninviting on a phone. With the right technique, they look clean, well-lit, and part of a complete home.

Two Photos of the Same House Tell Two Different Stories

There's no real competition between a professional listing photo and a phone photo of the same space. They don't communicate the same thing. One looks like someone trying to quickly squeeze as much money out of a sale as possible. The other looks like someone who knows their home is worth a buyer's investment and is willing to present it that way.

Buyers read that signal immediately, even if they couldn't articulate what they're responding to. The photo tells them something about how serious the seller is, how the agent operates, and whether this is a home worth their time.

What Buyers Actually Do With Bad Photos

They scroll right past. That's it. They don't click through to read the description. They don't schedule a showing to see if it looks better in person. They move on to the next listing in under a second.

Buyers are looking at listings to picture their lives in that space. They're imagining their furniture, their mornings, their family in these rooms. Uninviting photos, dark, crooked, with bad color, shut that imagination down before it has a chance to explore. You don't get a second chance at that first impression.

Does It Actually Help It Sell?

In my over a decade worth of experience, yes — consistently. I've seen well-photographed homes go under contract in days. I've also seen homes sit much longer than they should have, and when you look at the listing photos, the reason is usually obvious. Showing a house in the best possible light is almost always going to get it sold faster. That's not a guarantee, the market is what it is, but it gives every listing its best shot.

And speed matters. A home that sits accumulates questions. Buyers start wondering why it hasn't sold. Days on market becomes a negotiating point. Getting it right before it hits the market is the best way to avoid all of that.

Knoxville Is Not a Forgiving Market

Knoxville is one of the most competitive housing markets in the country right now. That makes professional photography not just a nice-to-have luxury, but a real competitive advantage. When buyers have a dozen options in a single neighborhood, the listings that look best get the attention.

Every type of property benefits from professional photography. It doesn't matter if it's a starter home or a luxury property. If it's on the market, it deserves to be presented at its best.

"I know people will scroll right past a poorly photographed listing. They want to picture their lives in that home. Uninviting photos turn them away before they even click."

And If You Still Want to Use Your Phone?

I understand why it's tempting. But when the angles are off and the house looks dark and uninviting, the listing pays for it in time on market. At that point, a reshoot is more expensive than doing it right the first time, and the first impression is already gone.

The best time to get professional photos is before the listing goes live. Not after it's been sitting for three weeks. Having the house prepped and shot before it hits the market is the single most effective thing a seller can do to give it the best chance of moving fast.
