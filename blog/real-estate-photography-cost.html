In the Knoxville market, professional real estate photography typically ranges from **$150 to $400** for a standard residential shoot, depending on the photographer's experience, what's included, and the size of the property. Here's a rough breakdown of what you can expect at each tier: **$75–$150 — Budget tier** Usually a newer photographer building their portfolio, or a side-hustler with a decent camera. You'll likely get basic photos, minimal editing, and inconsistent results. Turnaround can vary widely. **$150–$250 — Mid-range / professional** This is where you'll find experienced photographers who shoot real estate regularly, deliver consistent quality, and offer reliable turnaround times. Most Knoxville agents operate in this range for standard listings. **$250–$400+ — Premium / full-service** Experienced photographers offering more photos, advanced editing, and the option to add drone photography, video walk-throughs, 3D virtual tours, and floor plans. Worth every dollar on mid-to-high-value listings. I get this question occasionally, and I'll give you my honest answer: phone cameras have gotten genuinely impressive. But no, they're not a substitute for a professional photographer — and probably never will be. Here's why: photography isn't just about the camera. It's about angles, lighting, framing, timing, and knowing what has actually worked to get listings sold. That comes from years of experience, not megapixels. A professional knows to wait for the right exterior light. Knows which angle makes a small living room feel spacious. Knows how to balance the bright windows against the darker interior so both look natural. Knows what an MLS thumbnail looks like at 200 pixels wide and how to compose for that. A phone in the hands of someone without that experience will give you technically decent snapshots. It won't give you listing photos that make a buyer stop scrolling. --- ## When Should You Spend More? My honest take: every listing benefits from professional photography, including $180,000 starter homes. The agents who consistently dominate their market are the ones who treat every listing like it deserves to look its best — because their personal brand is attached to it just as much as the seller's home. That said, here's a practical guide to when the higher-tier packages make sense: **Spend more when:** - The listing is $400,000+ - The property has significant outdoor space, acreage, or architectural detail that benefits from drone coverage - It's a short-term rental that needs to compete on platforms like Airbnb - The seller has already invested in staging - You're trying to differentiate yourself from other agents marketing similar properties **The Standard package works well when:** - It's a straightforward residential listing - You're working with a tight commission structure - You need a reliable, fast turnaround without extras --- ## What Separates a $100 Photographer From a $300 Photographer? Beyond the obvious technical differences, here's what you're actually paying for at the higher end: **Experience with what works.** After hundreds of listings, a seasoned real estate photographer knows what makes buyers engage with a property online. That institutional knowledge shapes every shot. **Reliability.** A professional shows up on time, works efficiently, and delivers on schedule. A $100 photographer might be great — or might cancel the morning of your shoot because they got a better offer elsewhere. **Editing quality.** The difference between a competently shot photo and a great listing photo is largely in the edit. Sky replacements, exposure balance, window pull, color correction — these take skill and time to do well. **Communication.** Knowing how to prep a seller before the shoot, what to do when the property isn't ready (more on that in another post), and how to handle a rescheduled shoot professionally — that's all part of the service. --- ## The Bottom Line Starting with professional photography isn't a luxury — it's the foundation of a strong listing marketing strategy.
